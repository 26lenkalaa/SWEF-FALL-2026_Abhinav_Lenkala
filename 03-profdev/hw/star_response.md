# STAR response

**Question:** Tell me about a time you faced a challenge while working on a project or with a team. What did you do, and what was the result?

## Situation
From June 2025 to March 2026, I was an EMT student with Windsor EMS, where I logged more than 80 hours riding along on ambulance calls with working crews. One of the most physically demanding parts of the job was moving patients, especially heavier patients who had to be lifted onto a stretcher or carried down stairs. These lifts take several people working together, and on an ambulance crew there's no time to figure things out slowly.

## Task
As the newest and least experienced person on the crew, my job was to be a reliable set of hands during these moves. Early on I struggled with it. I would hesitate, wait to be told exactly where to stand, or lift slightly out of sync with everyone else. On a heavy lift, that kind of mismatch shifts weight unevenly onto the rest of the crew and makes the move less safe for the patient. I needed to become someone the crew could count on rather than someone they had to work around.


## Action

I realized the problem wasn't strength, it was communication. Instead of staying quiet and trying to read the situation, I started speaking up. Before a lift I would confirm my position and grip with the crew, and I made sure everyone agreed on who was calling the count. If I wasn't set, I said so instead of lifting anyway. I also paid close attention to how the experienced EMTs coordinated with each other, how they planned the path out of a room before moving, and how they talked through each step during the move. After calls, I asked them what I could have done better and applied that feedback on the next one.

## Result

Over the course of my ride-alongs, my lifts became noticeably smoother and more coordinated, and the crews began trusting me to take a full role in patient moves without needing to direct me step by step. The bigger lesson was that teamwork under pressure depends on clear, explicit communication, not on assuming everyone is on the same page. I bring that same habit to group projects now: I confirm roles early, say when I'm not ready or don't understand something, and ask for feedback instead of waiting for it.
